The move

Catalytic finance does more with the same money.

It ensures every rupee of philanthropic funding creates outsized impact by reaching more people as well as unlocking larger pools of public and private funding for underserved communities.

Delivered through two key principles, working independently or in tandem.

01

Multiplier effect.

The same money, used again and again.

By giving people the option to repay the grants they receive, the same funding can be recycled to support more and more people over time.

02

Leverage.

A little unlocks a lot.

A small amount of philanthropic capital addresses market challenges and unlocks vast amounts of public and private capital.

Our instruments

Three tools, each with a clear catalytic effect.

Used alone or combined into a single program, depending on the funder's goals and the community being served.

Returnable Grant

Up to 3×

Zero interest. Zero collateral.

Capital for people who have never taken a formal loan. Repayment is a moral obligation, not a legal one. Borrowers repay if they can, when they can, and the same pool funds the next person. 75–100% of borrowers have repaid across programs.

Best for: first-time, small-ticket capital for borrowers outside the formal financial system.

Interest Subvention

~6×

Affordable credit, where it matters.

Philanthropic capital brings down the interest on a commercial loan, making it affordable for low-income borrowers. Working capital, skilling, asset financing.

Best for: borrowers who can access loans but cannot afford the interest rates.

Credit Guarantee

Up to 20×

The biggest leverage in the toolkit.

Philanthropic capital absorbs a small portion of a bank's lending risk, unlocking commercial loans for borrowers who would otherwise be declined. ₹10,000 of guarantee can unlock ₹1–2 lakh in lending. Nil philanthropic guarantees have been invoked till March 2026.

Best for: borrowers who are rejected from formal loans due to high risk perception.

What makes it work

Key features of our approach.

Eight design principles that run through every program we build, from ideation to execution.

Designed for people with varying needs, backgrounds, and levels of credit readiness. No single tool is forced on every person.

  • Modular sequencing of grants, Returnable Grants, and Credit Guarantees tailored to each borrower’s journey and graduation timeline.
  • Integrated with livelihood programs, finance paired with financial literacy, skilling, entrepreneurship development, and market linkages.
  • Structured support for KYC, digital onboarding, bank disbursements, and UPI repayments, building people’s familiarity with formal finance.
  • Faster, more efficient, lower-cost delivery at scale, enabled by India's Digital Public Infrastructure and our own delivery systems.
  • Embedded measurement, evaluation, and learning systems enable robust tracking, outcome attribution, and continuous improvement.
  • People graduate from philanthropic support into stable, self-sustaining incomes, reducing long-term dependence on grants and external funding.
  • Delivery through government and private sector partnerships and enabling technology allows funding to reach more people at lower cost and greater speed.

What good catalytic finance should prove

  • Did the capital reach someone who would otherwise have been excluded?
  • What additional capital did it unlock?
  • Did the person’s income, resilience, enterprise or formal financial access improve?
  • How did the portfolio perform? repayment, utilisation, guarantee claims and credit progression
  • Could the model eventually operate with less philanthropic support?

In Maharashtra, 6,000 farmers across 12 Farmer Producer Organisations received a combination of returnable grants and guarantee-backed loans. The returnable grants, averaging ₹9,000 each, achieved a 95% repayment rate. Average annual farm income grew 159%. Average annual revenue grew 77%. On the guarantee side, ₹25 lakh of philanthropic capital unlocked ₹5 crore in formal bank loans, a 20x leverage, with not a single invocation to date. At the FPO level, average revenue tripled from ₹5 crore to ₹15 crore in three years.

Frequently asked questions

The questions our partners ask.

What is catalytic finance, and how is it different from a traditional grant?

+

A traditional grant is spent once. Catalytic finance uses the same philanthropic rupee to help multiple people by recycling it, by unlocking commercial capital from banks, or both.

Two examples of how this works in practice:

  • A Returnable Grant gives a citizen zero-interest, zero-collateral capital with only a moral obligation to repay. When they do, the same pool supports the next person. Our programs have demonstrated up to a 5× multiplier.
  • A Credit Guarantee uses philanthropic capital to absorb a small portion of a bank's lending risk. A ₹10,000 guarantee can unlock ₹1–₹2 lakh in loans for a farmer. As borrowers repay, the same guarantee supports more loans. We have unlocked up to 20× in formal lending this way.

What is a Returnable Grant, and how does it work in practice?

+

A Returnable Grant is zero-interest, zero-collateral capital for citizens who have never taken a formal loan. Typical amounts are ₹5,000 to ₹25,000, put towards skilling, working capital, or essential equipment.

Citizens voluntarily repay as their income stabilises, and in doing so, they build a track record of responsible financial behaviour. The repaid funds are redeployed to support the next citizen.

There is no legal obligation to repay. But citizens understand that when they do, the next person gets a chance. In our programs, that sense of collective responsibility translates into repayment rates of 75–100%.

How does a ₹10,000 credit guarantee really unlock 20× or ₹2 lakh in formal loans?

+

Banks are willing to lend, provided their risk is manageable. When a bank lends ₹2 lakh to a farmer with no formal credit history, its main concern is what happens if the farmer cannot repay.

A ₹10,000 philanthropic guarantee covers 5% of the loan amount, a small portion of the risk, but often enough to make the lending viable from the bank's perspective. The bank extends the full ₹2 lakh; the guarantee sits as a backstop.

Depending on the borrower segment and the bank's risk model, the guarantee ratio can range from 5% to 10%, producing the 10–20× leverage we see across our programs.

Is Catalytic Finance compliant with CSR and FCRA regulations?

+

Yes. Compliance is engineered into the design of every catalytic finance instrument we run, not added later.

Our instruments have been independently reviewed by the Centre for Advancement of Philanthropy and KPMG, and confirmed compliant with CSR (Companies Act Schedule VII), FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act, 2010), and RBI frameworks.

If your legal or compliance team has specific questions, we can share the written legal opinions and walk through them. Write to catalyticfinance@samhita.org.

Can UHNIs, family offices, or private donors use these instruments?

+

Yes. Corporate CSR teams, philanthropic foundations, UHNIs, family offices, and institutional funders can all deploy through the catalytic finance architecture, with the same leverage and compliance posture.

Typical structures for an individual donor, a family office, or a foundation include:

  • A corpus of Returnable Grants aligned to a cause you care about, for example, women entrepreneurs in a specific state, with repayment and redeployment reported on a defined cadence.
  • A First Loss Default Guarantee (FLDG) structured through a partner NBFC, unlocking commercial lending at 10–20× your commitment.
  • A blended commitment combining catalytic capital and grant support for an end-to-end program, with Samhita-CGF handling design, compliance, and operations.
